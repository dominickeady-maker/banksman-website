Construction management software in the UK typically costs anywhere from around £70–£150 a month + VAT for a small or mid-size firm using a flat-rate tool, up to several hundred or even thousands of pounds a month for enterprise platforms with per-seat pricing and custom quotes. The right figure depends far more on firm size and pricing model than on which specific product you pick.

## Why the range is so wide

**Flat-rate tools** charge one price for a set number of users, regardless of who's logging in. This suits smaller firms because the cost doesn't creep up as you add people, within the plan's limit.

**Per-seat pricing** charges per user per month. This can look cheap at a glance (£10–£30 per user + VAT is common) but scales directly with headcount — a 20-person firm paying £25 per seat + VAT is spending £500/month + VAT before add-ons.

**Enterprise / "contact sales" pricing** is common among the bigger platforms and is rarely published for a reason — it's usually negotiated per client, often bundled with onboarding, training, and support contracts, and can run into four figures a month for larger firms.

## What actually drives the price up

- **Number of users**, on any per-seat model
- **Number of modules** — estimating, CRM, bid management, scheduling, and accounting integrations are often priced as add-ons
- **Support tier** — priority or dedicated support is frequently a paid upgrade
- **Contract length** — annual contracts sometimes get a discount, but also lock you in before you know if the tool fits

## What a small or mid-size firm should actually expect to pay

For a firm of roughly 5–25 people running a handful of sites, a flat monthly rate of £70–£150 + VAT is realistic for a tool covering the core day-to-day needs — tasks, timesheets, materials, drawings, and site photos. That's a fraction of what per-seat or enterprise pricing tends to land at for the same headcount, because you're not paying for capability (multi-region reporting, complex bid workflows) you don't need at this size.

## A quick way to sanity-check a quote

Ask: does the price change if we add three more workers next month? If the answer is yes, the number you're being quoted today isn't the number you'll actually be paying in six months.
