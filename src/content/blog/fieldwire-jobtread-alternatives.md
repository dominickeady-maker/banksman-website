The main reason smaller building firms look for a Fieldwire or JobTread alternative is fit, not quality — both are capable platforms, but they're generally built and priced with larger firms or bigger project pipelines in mind. Firms running a handful of sites with a small team often want the same core capability (tasks, timesheets, photos, drawings) without the scale, per-seat pricing, or sales-led setup that comes with them.

## What Fieldwire and JobTread do well

Fieldwire is strong on plan markup and field coordination across larger, more complex projects. JobTread leans into job costing and the sales-to-project pipeline for firms managing multiple concurrent bids. Both are genuinely well-built for the firms they're aimed at — larger contractors managing several concurrent projects with dedicated project managers.

## Where the fit breaks down for smaller firms

- **Onboarding friction.** Getting started often means a demo call or guided setup rather than logging in and trying it immediately.
- **Pricing that scales with growth in a way that penalises it.** Per-seat pricing is manageable at 5 users and can become a real cost at 20+.
- **Feature depth you won't use.** Complex bid pipelines and multi-project cost tracking matter a lot to a contractor juggling ten tenders — much less to a firm running three known jobs with a stable team.

## What to look for in an alternative

1. **Try before you commit.** A live demo you can log into yourself beats a sales call every time.
2. **Flat, published pricing.** If the number isn't on the page, assume it's negotiable — which usually isn't in your favour.
3. **Coverage of the actual daily pain points**, not the full enterprise feature set: tasks and photo proof, timesheets (including daywork vs price work), materials requests, drawings on every phone, and messaging per site.
4. **Built for firms your size**, ideally by people who've actually run sites like yours — not scaled down from an enterprise product.
