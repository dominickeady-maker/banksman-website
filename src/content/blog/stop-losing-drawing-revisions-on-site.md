The most reliable way to stop losing drawing revisions on site is to keep a single accessible source for the current version — on every phone, downloadable on site — rather than relying on printed copies or emailed PDFs that get overtaken by later revisions without anyone removing the old ones.

## Why this keeps happening

It's rarely carelessness. It's structural:

- **Printed drawings don't update themselves.** Once it's on paper and pinned up in the site cabin, it stays there until someone remembers to swap it — which might be days after the revision changed.
- **Email chains bury the latest version.** The current drawing is "somewhere in that thread" — usually not the last message, because someone replied "thanks" after it.
- **Nobody's clearly responsible for distribution.** The person who receives the revised drawing from the architect or client isn't always the person who needs to get it in front of the person laying blockwork.

## What it costs when it goes wrong

Working from an outdated drawing isn't a paperwork issue — it's rework, wasted materials, and in the worst case, work that has to be undone and redone at your cost, not the client's. It also erodes trust: a client who finds out the site was working from a superseded drawing starts questioning everything else too.

## What actually fixes it

1. **One current version, accessible from site.** Not "the version I printed last week" — the actual current revision, viewable on a phone on site.
2. **Old versions removed from circulation, not just superseded.** If the outdated copy is still pinned up or still the top result in an email thread, it's still a risk.
3. **A clear moment when a new revision "goes live."** Everyone on site should be able to tell, without asking, whether they're looking at the latest version.
4. **No printing as the default.** Printing isn't wrong for a specific use (marking up on paper, say), but it shouldn't be the way drawings are distributed day to day.
