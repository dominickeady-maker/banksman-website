Every job I’ve run started the same way: a new WhatsApp group, everyone added, and for the first week it works brilliantly. By week three there are two groups (one with the client, one without), a separate chat with the groundworker, photos of the wrong elevation, and a voice note about the RSJ that nobody can find when the building inspector asks.

WhatsApp isn’t the problem. It’s brilliant for a quick “running ten minutes late”. The problem is that we use it to run the whole job, and it was never built for that.

## Why group chats fall apart on site

**Everything is in one long scroll.** Tasks, photos, material orders, hours and banter all sit in the same thread. If you want the photo of the cavity trays from Tuesday, you’re scrolling past forty memes to find it.

**Nothing has an owner.** “Can someone order more wall ties?” gets three thumbs up and no wall ties. A message isn’t a task, and a thumbs up isn’t a yes.

**There’s no record that stands up.** When a client disputes an extra, or a sub says he did eleven hours not eight, a chat history is a poor defence. Photos lose their context, and hours were never written down properly in the first place.

**The latest drawing is a guess.** Rev B gets sent to the group, then Rev C gets emailed to the office, and the lads on site are building off whichever one they saw last.

**People leave, and the history leaves with them.** When a lad changes phones or a sub leaves the group, whatever they posted is gone for good.

## What actually needs to move

You don’t need to move everything. Keep WhatsApp for the chat. Move the five things that cost you money when they go wrong:

1. **Tasks.** Who’s doing what, on which site, and whether it’s done.
2. **Photo proof.** Photos tied to the task and the site, with a date on them.
3. **Hours.** Daywork and price work logged on the day, not remembered on Friday.
4. **Materials.** Requested by the lad who needs them, approved by the person who pays.
5. **Drawings.** On every phone, labelled by version, so nobody’s squinting at an old print.

Get those five out of the group chat and most of the arguments go with them.

## How to switch without a mutiny

This is the part most software companies get wrong. They build something for the office and expect the lads to fill it in. That never works. If it takes longer than a WhatsApp message, it won’t get used.

**Start with one site, not all of them.** Pick your most organised job and your most reasonable foreman. Prove it there first.

**Only ask the lads for two things at first.** Mark the task done and take a photo. That’s it. Once that’s a habit, add hours.

**Make it quicker than the old way.** If logging hours takes them longer than texting you, they won’t bother. It should be a couple of taps on site, not a form at the end of the week.

**Stop answering the old way.** This is the hard bit. When someone sends a photo to the group, reply “stick it on the job so it’s saved”. Do it for two weeks and it sticks.

**Keep the group for chat.** Don’t ban WhatsApp. “What time’s the skip coming?” can stay in the group. Just move the records out of it.

## What good looks like after a month

- You can open any site and see what’s done, what’s next and who’s on it, without ringing round.
- Friday hours are already in, and they were logged on the day by the person who did them.
- Every task has a dated photo against it, ready if there’s ever a dispute.
- Material requests come to you to approve, instead of turning up on an invoice you didn’t expect.
- Drawings are on every phone, labelled by version, instead of pinned up in the cabin or buried in the chat.
